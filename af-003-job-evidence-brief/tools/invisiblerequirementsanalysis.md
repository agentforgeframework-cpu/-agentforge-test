## Purpose

Identify a small number of defensible hypotheses about capabilities, perspectives, or experience that may matter to an opportunity even though they are not stated directly in the job posting.

The analysis combines:

- explicit job requirements;
- verified employer context;
- validated candidate evidence.

Invisible Requirements are hypotheses to investigate, not facts to assert.

Internal Working Analysis / Not Intended for External Distribution

Provide:

- original job posting;
- completed Job Evidence Brief;

- validated candidate evidence used by the Job Evidence Brief.

Do not perform new employer research inside this tool unless the human explicitly authorizes correction of a missing factual point.

If the Due Diligence Brief is incomplete or outdated, stop and request an updated brief.

## Working Definition

> An Invisible Requirement is a capability, perspective, or form of experience that is not stated directly in the job posting but is reasonably suggested by the employer's verified business context, strategic direction, operating environment, or probable underlying need.

The term does not imply concealment.

An unstated need may exist because:

- the posting uses a standard template;
- the role has not been fully defined;
- the capability is assumed internally;
- company strategy has moved faster than the posting;
- different stakeholders understand the role differently;
- the posting describes immediate work but not likely future direction.

---

## Inference Strength

Use only these labels.

### Strongly Supported

The inference is supported by multiple credible signals, such as the job posting plus documented company strategy, operating conditions, or repeated executive statements.

### Reasonably Suggested

The inference is supported by one or more credible signals and is useful enough to investigate or prepare for, but it is not confirmed.

### Speculative

The inference is plausible but weakly supported.

Speculative items should normally be excluded from the final analysis. Preserve them only when the human specifically requests a broader hypothesis list.

Inference strength describes the strength of the hypothesis, not the strength of the candidate.

---

## Analytical Categories

Consider only categories supported by the inputs.

### Strategic Direction

Capabilities suggested by documented priorities, investments, acquisitions, transformation plans, or technology direction.

### Operating Environment

Capabilities likely to matter because of how the organization functions.

### Underlying Business Problem

Capabilities suggested by the probable operational reason the role exists.

### Organizational Expectations

Capabilities commonly expected at the stated level, provided the job posting or verified context supports the connection.

### Future-State Expectations

Capabilities that may become important as the role or organization develops.

### Risk and Governance

Capabilities implied by the consequences of failure, including continuity, auditability, security, regulation, data integrity, or human oversight.

---

## Evidence Relationship Labels

Use these labels internally when evaluating candidate evidence.

### Direct

The candidate performed substantially the same work or demonstrated the same capability.

### Adjacent

The candidate worked in a closely related function, environment, or technology.

### Transferable

The candidate demonstrated a capability that may carry over, but the target context differs materially.

### Gap

No reliable supporting candidate evidence was identified.

These labels may be included in working notes but need not appear in downstream external documents.

---

## Workflow

1. Confirm the explicit requirements already captured in the Job Evidence Brief.
2. Review the verified context in the Due Diligence Brief.
3. Identify signals that may imply additional capability needs.
4. For every proposed Invisible Requirement, identify the nearest explicit language in the posting and state the additional unstated expectation being inferred. If the posting already states substantially the same capability, or no meaningful additional expectation can be identified, discard the item.
5. Eliminate fashionable or generic capabilities unsupported by the employer context.
6. Identify no more than five defensible Invisible Requirements.
7. State the factual basis for each inference.
8. Match each inference to validated candidate evidence.
9. Assign an inference-strength label.
10. Convert remaining uncertainty into questions the candidate can test with the employer.

## Analysis

| Possible Invisible Requirement | Basis for the Inference | Supporting Candidate Evidence | Evidence Relationship | Inference Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hypothesis | Nearest explicit posting language; additional unstated expectation; verified context supporting the inference | Validated evidence or clear gap | Direct / Adjacent / Transferable / Gap | Strongly Supported / Reasonably Suggested |

Limit the final table to the strongest three to five items.

## Questions to Test

For each important hypothesis, provide a neutral question that could confirm, refine, or reject it during networking or interviews.

Examples:

- How is this role expected to support the organization's current modernization priorities?
- Which operational problems are most important for the person in this role to address first?
- How does the team balance automation with human review in high-impact decisions?

## Implications for Candidate Preparation

Identify where the findings may affect:

- interview stories;
- resume emphasis;
- Qualifications Brief content;
- portfolio selection;
- outreach questions;
- training or preparation.

Do not make final application decisions for the candidate.

---

## Required Guardrails

The analysis shall:

- distinguish explicit requirements from inferred ones;
- exclude any proposed item that merely repeats or rephrases an explicit posting requirement;
- identify the factual basis for every inference;
- use cautious language;
- avoid assuming internal employer intent;
- avoid treating public strategy as proof of the hiring manager's priorities;
- avoid adding fashionable technologies merely because they are popular;
- require genuine candidate evidence before claiming alignment;
- exclude weak or decorative connections;
- never claim that an Invisible Requirement is mandatory unless the employer confirms it.

Preferred wording:

- "The company's direction suggests that..."
- "The operating environment may place additional value on..."
- "Although not stated directly, the role may benefit from..."
- "This appears relevant to the organization's broader direction..."

Avoid:

- "The employer secretly wants..."
- "The real requirement is..."
- "The posting failed to mention..."
- "This is definitely required..."

---

## Downstream Use

Invisible Requirements findings may influence:

- candidate preparation;
- resume evidence selection;
- Qualifications Brief emphasis;
- interview questions;
- networking strategy;
- portfolio selection.

Do not normally use the label "Invisible Requirements" in external documents.

The Qualifications Brief may use selected insights only after converting them into supportable, professionally appropriate candidate positioning.

---

## Success Criteria

A successful analysis should:

- add useful insight beyond the literal posting;
- show what new unstated expectation is inferred beyond the nearest explicit posting language;
- remain traceable to verified context;
- identify only a few defensible hypotheses;
- clearly communicate uncertainty;
- help the candidate prepare better questions and evidence;
- avoid exposing speculation as employer fact.
