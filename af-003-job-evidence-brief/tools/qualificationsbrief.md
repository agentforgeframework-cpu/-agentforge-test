## Purpose

Create a concise, recruiter-facing qualifications brief that explains why a candidate is well suited for a specific opportunity.

The brief supplements the resume.

It does not replace the resume, duplicate the resume, or serve as an ATS submission document.

The tool converts evidence identified through the AF-003 Job Evidence Brief into a clear, targeted document that a recruiter or hiring manager can review quickly and forward when appropriate.

---

## Classification

```text
External Candidate Representation / Shareable After Human Review
```

The Qualifications Brief is the disclosure boundary in the AF-003 workflow. It may use selected findings from upstream tools, but it shall not expose private speculation or present inferred employer needs as confirmed facts.

---

## Recommended Sequence

1. Review the job posting.
2. Run the AF-003 Job Evidence Brief.
3. Validate the evidence against source documents.
4. When useful, run the Due Diligence Brief.
5. When useful, run the Invisible Requirements Analysis.
6. Run the AF-003 Qualifications Brief.
7. Review and edit the document for personal accuracy, disclosure, and voice.
8. Export the final version to PDF, Microsoft Word, or another suitable file type.

---

## Inputs

### Required

Provide:

* The specific job posting
* The completed AF-003 Job Evidence Brief
* The candidate resume or career-history documents used to create the JEB

### Optional

Provide any of the following when available:

* LinkedIn profile
* Portfolio or project-navigation page
* Selected project descriptions
* Case studies
* Publications
* Presentations
* Recruiter or hiring-manager information
* Preferred document title
* Employer or recruiting-firm name
* Completed Due Diligence Brief
* Completed Invisible Requirements Analysis

---

## Document Purpose

The completed document should help a recruiter or hiring manager answer:

> Why is this candidate especially relevant to this assignment?

It should make the candidate easier to understand, represent, and present to the client.

The document should demonstrate relevance through selected experience rather than broad claims.

---

## Output Type

Create a role-specific qualifications brief suitable for:

* Recruiter follow-up
* Hiring-manager review
* Consulting engagements
* Short-term contracts
* Specialized technical roles
* Senior roles requiring interpretation of a broad career history

Default length:

* Two pages maximum

Default format:

* Clean professional layout
* Candidate name and contact information
* Opportunity-specific title
* Short sections
* Strong visual hierarchy
* Limited use of bullets
* No dense requirement matrix
* No ATS optimization

---

## Naming Convention

Preferred document title:

**[Role or Capability] Qualifications Brief**

Examples:

* Enterprise Architecture Qualifications Brief
* AI Solutions Qualifications Brief
* Emerging Technology Qualifications Brief
* Analytics Modernization Qualifications Brief
* Current-State Architecture Assessment Qualifications

Avoid titles such as:

* Job Evidence Brief
* Candidate Evidence Report
* Job Fit Analysis
* Applicant Scorecard
* Resume Supplement
* AI Analysis
* Role Research Report

The Job Evidence Brief is an internal evidence-development artifact.

The Qualifications Brief is an external candidate-representation artifact.

---

## Upstream Context Rules

Do not independently invent employer context or unstated needs.

Use only:

- the job posting;
- validated candidate source material;
- the completed Job Evidence Brief;
- factual findings from the Due Diligence Brief;
- selected, human-approved insights from the Invisible Requirements Analysis.

When an upstream Invisible Requirements finding is used, rewrite it as professionally appropriate candidate positioning. Do not state that the employer "really wants" something or treat an unconfirmed hypothesis as fact.

The Qualifications Brief should communicate value, not expose the candidate's private analytical work.

---

## Recommended Structure

### Header

Include:

* Candidate name
* City and state
* Email
* Telephone number
* LinkedIn profile
* Optional direct portfolio address

Do not overload the header.

Place optional portfolio or project-navigation links near the end of the document unless the portfolio is central to the role.

---

### Title

Use an opportunity-specific title.

Example:

**TriCom Technical Services — Enterprise Architecture Qualifications Brief**

Subtitle example:

**Current-State Assessment | Application Dependencies | Modernization Planning**

---

### 1. The Assignment

Write a short summary of the apparent business problem behind the posting.

This section should explain what the employer or client appears to need.

Do not merely repeat the job description.

Identify the probable operational objective, such as:

* understanding the current environment;
* reducing modernization risk;
* documenting institutional knowledge;
* stabilizing an inherited system;
* improving adoption;
* preparing for migration;
* supporting a short-term capability gap.

Use cautious language when employer intent is not explicit.

Preferred wording:

* “The assignment appears to require…”
* “The client needs someone who can…”
* “The central challenge is likely…”

---

### 2. Direct Alignment

Select three to five major areas of alignment from the Job Evidence Brief.

Examples:

* Current-state discovery
* Application and data mapping
* Modernization impact analysis
* Stakeholder communication
* Governance and risk
* Training and adoption
* Operational stabilization

Each alignment statement should be brief.

Do not list every requirement in the posting.

---

### 3. Representative Technology or Domain Landscape

Include only when it helps the reader understand the candidate’s range.

Use a short line or compact list.

Examples:

* Platforms
* Industries
* Enterprise systems
* Data technologies
* Architecture environments

Do not create a complete technical inventory.

The resume remains the primary source for comprehensive skills.

---

### 4. Proposed Approach

Describe how the candidate would approach the assignment.

This section must be clearly framed as a proposed method, not as proof that every listed activity has already been performed.

Preferred heading:

**Proposed Approach for This Assignment**

Possible sequence:

1. Establish the business context
2. Discover the current state
3. Trace dependencies
4. Validate findings
5. Produce usable deliverables

The approach should be customized to the opportunity.

Do not promise deliverables that the candidate cannot reasonably produce.

---

### 5. Selected Relevant Engagements

Select three to five examples from the Job Evidence Brief.

Each example should include:

* Organization or engagement
* Short engagement title
* Three to five evidence-based bullets
* One concise relevance statement

Example format:

**PINNACLE FINANCIAL PARTNERS**
*Undocumented Post-Merger Reporting Environment*

* Entered a critical reporting environment with no usable documentation.
* Reverse-engineered daily reporting processes.
* Rebuilt and stabilized production workflows.
* Created validation controls and audit trails.
* Delivered a documented framework to permanent staff.

**Relevance:** Current-state discovery, proprietary applications, post-merger complexity, documentation, and knowledge transfer.

Select examples based on relevance, not prestige or recency alone.

---

### 6. Additional Relevant Experience

Include three to five short supporting examples that are relevant but do not require full case-study treatment.

Avoid repeating content already used in the selected engagements.

---

### 7. Representative Work

Include an optional portfolio or project-navigation link when the linked material strengthens the application.

Preferred label:

**Representative Work Portfolio**

Example:

Architecture concepts, modernization research, technical documentation, presentations, and selected projects:

[Direct portfolio address]

Prefer a direct, recognizable URL over a shortened link when possible.

Do not include a portfolio link if the destination is unfinished, difficult to navigate, overly broad, or unrelated to the role.

---

### 8. Fit for the Engagement

Close with a concise statement connecting the candidate’s experience to the central business problem.

Acknowledge a meaningful domain gap when doing so strengthens credibility.

Example:

> My background is not specifically within an architecture, engineering, or construction firm. My experience is directly relevant, however, to the central challenge of this engagement: understanding a complex enterprise environment before leadership makes irreversible modernization decisions.

Do not apologize for minor gaps.

Do not claim perfect alignment.

---

## Evidence Rules

Every factual claim must be traceable to candidate-provided source material.

Do not:

* invent projects;
* expand responsibility beyond the source;
* imply formal authority that was not documented;
* convert participation into leadership;
* convert review into ownership;
* convert documentation into reconstruction;
* convert migration support into enterprise-wide transformation;
* add tools, frameworks, certifications, industries, or deliverables not found in the source materials.

When wording is broader than the source, narrow it.

Prefer:

* “supported”
* “contributed”
* “assessed”
* “documented”
* “worked with”
* “helped determine”

Use stronger verbs only when supported:

* “led”
* “directed”
* “owned”
* “architected”
* “transformed”

---

## Evidence Audit

Before finalizing, perform a claim-by-claim audit.

Classify each statement as:

* Directly supported
* Reasonable synthesis
* Proposed approach
* Unsupported or overstated

Revise or remove unsupported and overstated claims.

Clearly distinguish:

* completed work;
* inferred capability;
* proposed approach;
* probable employer need.

---

## Pre-Output Acceptance Gate

Before producing the final shareable brief, confirm:

```text
Every candidate claim is traceable:
Every employer-context claim is sourced or taken directly from the posting:
Every proposed approach is clearly labeled:
No private hypothesis is presented as employer fact:
Any meaningful gap is handled honestly:
Candidate has not yet approved sharing:
```

If a material claim is unsupported or overstated, revise or remove it before final output.

The AI may prepare a draft. The human decides whether the document is accurate and appropriate to share.

---

## Resume Separation Rules

The Qualifications Brief must not become a second resume.

Do not include:

* complete employment chronology;
* full skills inventory;
* education details unless directly relevant;
* every accomplishment;
* every technology;
* standard resume summary language;
* ATS keywords inserted without context.

The resume establishes career history.

The Qualifications Brief explains relevance.

---

## Recruiter Use

The completed brief should help a recruiter:

* understand the candidate quickly;
* explain the candidate’s fit to the client;
* prepare a candidate-submission summary;
* identify relevant examples for interviews;
* distinguish the candidate from applicants with similar titles;
* forward a polished supporting document when appropriate.

The brief should not instruct the recruiter how to represent the candidate.

It should give the recruiter useful, accurate material.

---

## Quality Review

Before delivery, verify:

* The title matches the opportunity.
* Headers and footers use the same document name.
* Names, dates, employers, tools, and accomplishments are accurate.
* Spelling and grammar are correct.
* No unsupported claims remain.
* The document is no longer than two pages unless the user explicitly approves more.
* The document reads smoothly when viewed independently from the resume.
* The resume and brief complement rather than duplicate each other.
* The portfolio link works and adds value.
* The final file name clearly identifies the candidate and document.

Preferred file name:

`Candidate_Name_Role_Qualifications_Brief.pdf`

## Success Criteria

The output should help answer:

> Why should this recruiter or hiring manager take a closer look at this candidate for this specific assignment?

The result should be:

* accurate;
* concise;
* role-specific;
* evidence-based;
* easy to scan;
* professionally presented;
* useful alongside a resume;
* safe for a recruiter to forward.
