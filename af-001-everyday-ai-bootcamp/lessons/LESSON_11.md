# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Calibrate reliance according to context and consequence.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 12–15 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A reliance decision with a reason and next action.  
**Memory anchor:** **Reliance is a dial, not a switch.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** Repeating a full verification lesson, abstract risk theory, legal or clinical decision-making, and blanket trust or distrust.

The learner chooses among:

- use;
- revise;
- verify further;
- consult a qualified person;
- reject;
- stop using AI for the task.

# Begin the Lesson

Say:

> AI output is not simply trusted or distrusted. You may rely on it differently depending on consequence, evidence, uncertainty, and your ability to judge it.

# EXPLAIN — Match Reliance to the Situation

Ask:

- What is the result being used for?
- What happens if it is wrong?
- What evidence supports it?
- Can I judge the quality?
- Is qualified authority required?
- What responsibility remains mine?

# DEMONSTRATE — Same Tool, Different Reliance

1. AI suggests three fictional character names.  
   Use if they fit.

2. AI drafts a customer email.  
   Revise and review before sending.

3. AI reports a current deadline.  
   Verify from the official source.

4. AI interprets a serious symptom.  
   Consult qualified healthcare help; do not rely on AI for the decision.

5. AI repeatedly invents facts for a task you cannot evaluate.  
   Reject or stop.

Say:

> The output may be fluent in every case. The reliance decision changes because the use and consequence change.

# GUIDE — Choose the Reliance Level

Present:

- a casual brainstorming list;
- instructions for changing a workplace system;
- a summary of a public document the learner has also read;
- a confident answer containing no source for a consequential claim.

Ask the learner to choose a reliance action for each and explain why.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 1 — Reliance Calibrated

Do not require the same answer in every context. Require a defensible reason tied to consequence, evidence, and ability to judge.

# GUIDE — Separate “Good Output” From “Safe to Use”

Say:

> An output can be well written and still deserve low reliance. A rough output can still contain a useful starting point.

Ask the learner:

> Describe one case where you would use an imperfect AI result after revision, and one case where you would reject a polished result.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 2 — Fluency Separated From Reliability

# ENABLE — Make a Reliance Decision

Present:

> AI gives a clear, confident recommendation for a task that matters to you. You recognize some of the topic, but not enough to evaluate every claim. One claim could materially affect your action.

Say:

> Choose: use, revise, verify further, consult, reject, or stop. Explain what evidence or authority would change your reliance and what responsibility remains yours.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 3 — Independent Reliance Judgment

Completion evidence:

- reliance action selected;
- consequence and evidence considered;
- ability to judge considered;
- next action stated;
- final responsibility retained.

# Lesson Recap

Say:

> Do not ask only, “Is AI trustworthy?” Ask, “How much reliance does this result deserve for this use?”
