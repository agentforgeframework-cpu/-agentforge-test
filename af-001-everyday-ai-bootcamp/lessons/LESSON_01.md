# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Refine an AI result through follow-up interaction.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 12–15 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A before-and-after result with the learner’s reason for the revision.  
**Memory anchor:** **The first result is a starting point.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** Prompt formulas, prompt engineering terminology, advanced settings, and long theory about how models work.

Success requires the learner to:

1. make a simple request;
2. inspect the first response;
3. identify one meaningful improvement;
4. ask for that revision;
5. decide whether the revision is better.

# Begin the Lesson

Say:

> Today you will improve an AI result through conversation. You do not need a perfect first request. The first result gives you something to inspect and improve.

Then continue directly to the demonstration.

# EXPLAIN — First Is Not Final

AI often gives a usable-looking response quickly. Quick and polished are not the same as fit for your purpose.

A productive habit is:

> **Ask → Inspect → Refine**

Do not explain further before the demonstration.

# DEMONSTRATE — One Useful Follow-Up

Show this compact example:

**First request**

```text
Write a reminder about tomorrow’s neighborhood meeting.
```

**Possible first result**

```text
Reminder: The neighborhood meeting is tomorrow. We hope to see you there.
```

Model the inspection aloud:

- It does not include the time.
- It does not include the location.
- It sounds generic.

Model one follow-up:

```text
Revise it for a friendly text message. Add 7:00 PM and the community room. Keep it under 35 words.
```

Then say:

> The second request did not start over. It improved the result by naming what was missing and what should change.

# GUIDE — Improve a First Result

Invite the learner to choose one safe task:

- a short reminder;
- a two-sentence explanation;
- a simple list of ideas;
- a brief announcement;
- another harmless topic.

Ask the learner to make a simple first request now.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 1 — First Result Received

After the learner receives a result, ask only:

1. What is one thing that would make this more useful?
2. What exact change will you request?

Do not identify the improvement first.

Offer a hint only when needed:

> Look for something missing, too long, too vague, wrong for the audience, or in the wrong format.

Stop and wait.

When the learner has chosen an improvement, ask them to send the follow-up request.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 2 — Revision Requested

Ask:

- What changed?
- Is the revision better for your purpose?
- Would you use it, refine it again, or stop?

Accept any reasoned answer. Do not require endless refinement.

# ENABLE — Do It With Less Help

Ask the learner to make one new, small request of their own choosing.

Give only these instructions:

> Inspect the first result. Choose one meaningful improvement. Request the revision. Then tell me whether the revision is better and why.

Do not suggest the improvement unless the learner asks for help.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 3 — Independent Refinement

The lesson is complete when the learner has independently:

- identified a weakness or improvement;
- requested a specific revision;
- compared the two results;
- made a fit-for-purpose judgment.
