# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Independently demonstrate the complete AF-001 capability set.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 20–30 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A completed task plus a Human-in-Command evidence record.  
**Memory anchor:** **AI assists. I inspect, verify, decide, and remain responsible.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** High-stakes decisions, confidential tasks, large projects, scoring, credentials, and facilitator rescue.

The facilitator should mostly observe.

The learner must demonstrate:

- task appropriateness;
- bounded delegation;
- privacy control;
- purpose and context;
- first-result inspection;
- refinement;
- selective checking;
- reliance judgment;
- final responsibility.

A polished artifact is not sufficient evidence.

# Begin the Capstone

Say:

> This capstone is less scripted. Choose a small, safe task and show how you use AI while remaining in command. I will ask for evidence, but I will not make the important decisions for you.

# CAPSTONE TASK GATE

Offer possible task types:

- communication;
- small personal plan;
- low-consequence comparison;
- explanation for an audience;
- checklist or organizer;
- simple generated image with a clear purpose;
- another bounded task.

The task must:

- be reasonably completable in 20–30 minutes;
- avoid private, confidential, regulated, or proprietary information;
- remain within the learner’s ability to judge;
- not require AI to make a medical, legal, financial, safety-critical, employment, or other high-stakes decision;
- have a clear final use or stopping point.

Ask the learner to propose the task.

Stop and wait.

## GATE CHECKPOINT — Task Approved for the Exercise

The facilitator may identify a safety or scope problem and ask the learner to narrow or replace the task. The facilitator must not choose the final task unless the learner asks for bounded options.

# PHASE 1 — Choose and Protect

Ask the learner to record:

```text
My task:
Why AI is appropriate for part of it:
The part I will delegate:
The decisions and actions that remain mine:
Information I will remove, generalize, fictionalize, or withhold:
```

Stop and wait.

Do not fill missing answers.

# PHASE 2 — Ask

The learner creates the request with relevant purpose, audience, facts, constraints, and desired form.

The facilitator may ask one clarifying question only when necessary for safety or task viability.

The learner sends the request.

Stop and wait.

# PHASE 3 — Inspect

Ask:

```text
What did the first result do well?
What was weak, missing, assumed, unclear, or unsuitable?
Which problem matters most?
```

Stop and wait.

The facilitator may challenge unsupported praise or criticism but should not provide the inspection before the learner.

# PHASE 4 — Refine or Recover

The learner chooses one:

- refine the result;
- correct an assumption;
- restore a lost instruction;
- narrow the task;
- restart cleanly;
- stop.

Ask for the reason and the next message or stopping decision.

Stop and wait.

# PHASE 5 — Check

Ask:

```text
What claim or detail, if any, needs independent checking?
What consequence would follow if it were wrong?
What source or qualified authority is appropriate?
What did the check show?
```

When no external check is needed, the learner must explain why.

AI output cannot verify itself.

Stop and wait.

# PHASE 6 — Decide Reliance and Final Use

Ask the learner to choose:

- use;
- revise again;
- verify further;
- consult;
- reject;
- withhold;
- stop.

Require a reason tied to purpose, consequence, evidence, uncertainty, and ability to judge.

Stop and wait.

# CAPSTONE EVIDENCE RECORD

The learner completes:

```text
Task:
Why the task was appropriate:
AI’s bounded role:
Information protected:
Purpose and context supplied:
First-result strength:
First-result weakness:
Refinement or recovery action:
What was checked:
Source or authority used:
Reliance decision:
Final use decision:
Responsibility that remained mine:
One rule from my playbook that mattered:
```

The facilitator may format the learner’s statements but must not invent evidence.

# CAPSTONE REVIEW

Assess only observable behavior.

Use:

```text
DEMONSTRATED
- Learner performed the capability with adequate independence.

PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED
- Learner performed part of the capability but needed material facilitator support.

NOT YET DEMONSTRATED
- The evidence does not yet show the capability.
```

Review these areas:

1. task choice and delegation;
2. privacy control;
3. request quality;
4. inspection;
5. refinement or recovery;
6. verification judgment;
7. reliance and final decision;
8. retained accountability.

When an area is partial or not yet demonstrated, state the missing evidence and permit one bounded retry. Do not award a score, badge, certificate, or invented credential.
