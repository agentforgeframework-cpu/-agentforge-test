# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Control what information enters the AI system.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 12–15 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A privacy-safe version of a realistic request with an explanation of the changes.  
**Memory anchor:** **Protect first. Prompt second.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** Legal advice, organization-specific policy interpretation, comprehensive data classification, and security engineering.

The learner must make the disclosure decisions.

# Begin the Lesson

Say:

> Privacy decisions happen before information enters the AI system. Today you will edit a request so the AI receives what it needs—and not what it does not.

# EXPLAIN — Six Choices Before Sharing

For each detail, the learner may:

- retain it;
- remove it;
- replace it;
- generalize it;
- fictionalize it;
- withhold the task.

The safest useful request is not always the most detailed request.

# DEMONSTRATE — Keep the Need, Remove the Identity

Original idea:

```text
Write an email to my supervisor, Maria Alvarez, at Northside Medical Center. Tell her patient James R. Wilson, medical record 482991, complained about...
```

Model the decision:

- Names and identifiers are unnecessary.
- Employer and patient details create avoidable risk.
- The writing pattern can be practiced with a fictionalized situation.

Safer request:

```text
Using a fictional healthcare workplace example, draft a calm email to a supervisor reporting that a customer raised a service concern. Do not include names, account numbers, medical details, or other identifying information.
```

Say:

> The task remained possible after the sensitive details were removed.

# GUIDE — Edit Before Entry

Present this unsent request:

```text
Summarize my employee Jordan Lee’s performance problems using these dates, customer names, disciplinary notes, home address, and medical leave information.
```

Ask the learner to classify each type of information:

- retain;
- remove;
- replace;
- generalize;
- fictionalize;
- withhold.

Stop and wait.

Do not perform the screening first.

## CHECKPOINT 1 — Disclosure Decisions Made

Ask:

> Can the underlying task be practiced safely? Rewrite it so the AI has only what it needs.

Stop and wait.

A suitable result might use a fictional employee, generic performance categories, no customer identities, no home address, and no medical information. The learner’s wording controls.

# GUIDE — Permission Matters Too

Say:

> Removing names does not automatically make information safe. Details can still be confidential, proprietary, regulated, or recognizable.

Ask the learner:

1. Am I authorized to use this information here?
2. Is the information necessary?
3. Could a generic or fictional example work?
4. Would I be comfortable if this input were reviewed later?

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 2 — Safe Alternative Chosen

Present three tasks and ask which can proceed:

- Rewrite a public announcement.
- Analyze confidential customer records without authorization.
- Practice a difficult conversation using a fictional scenario.

The learner should explain each choice.

# ENABLE — Protect a Request Independently

Offer one safe exercise:

```text
Original draft:
"Help me ask for advice about a conflict involving [real names], [employer details],
[private messages], and [personal history]."
```

Say:

> Convert this into a useful AI request without exposing unnecessary personal or organizational information. Then list what you retained, removed, replaced, generalized, fictionalized, or withheld.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 3 — Privacy Gate Applied

Completion evidence:

- unnecessary details identified;
- authorization and necessity considered;
- safer request created or task withheld;
- changes explained.

# Lesson Recap

Say:

> Do not wait for the AI to protect your information. Decide what may be shared before you send it.
