# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Direct and critically inspect a generated visual result.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 15â€“17 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A generated image or visual plan, an inspection note, and a revision decision.  
**Memory anchor:** **Generated images are created content, not evidence.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** Professional graphic design, image editing mastery, video, copyrighted-style imitation, and deceptive media.

If image generation is unavailable, use the fallback activity in this file. Do not substitute a product tour.

# Begin the Lesson

Say:

> Visual AI follows the same basic pattern as text: state the purpose, inspect the first result, refine it, and decide whether it fits. A generated image is created contentâ€”not proof that anything happened.

# EXPLAIN â€” Describe the Job, Then Inspect the Picture

Useful visual direction may include:

- purpose;
- subject;
- setting;
- composition;
- mood;
- important inclusions or exclusions;
- format.

The first image may contain mismatches, missing elements, visual errors, or unintended implications.

# DEMONSTRATE â€” One Visual Revision

Example purpose:

> A friendly fictional illustration for a neighborhood garden club announcement.

First direction:

```text
Create a simple, cheerful illustration of neighbors working in a community garden. Horizontal layout, room for a title on the left, no logos, no readable signs.
```

Model inspection:

- Does it look welcoming?
- Is there room for the title?
- Are unwanted words or logos present?
- Are hands, tools, and plants visually plausible?
- Does it imply a real documented event?

Model one revision:

```text
Keep the horizontal composition, but move the people to the right, leave more empty space on the left, and remove any text-like markings.
```

# GUIDE â€” Create a Safe Image

Offer choices:

- a fictional community event illustration;
- a simple nature scene;
- a harmless educational concept;
- a decorative image for a personal project;
- another safe purpose.

Ask the learner to choose one and state:

1. purpose;
2. subject;
3. two important visual requirements;
4. one exclusion.

Stop and wait.

Ask the learner to generate the image.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 1 â€” First Image Created

Ask the learner to inspect:

- what fits;
- what does not;
- what is missing;
- what looks misleading or implausible;
- what should change.

Do not identify the defects first.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 2 â€” Revision Directed

Ask the learner to request one meaningful revision.

After the revision, ask:

- Is it suitable for the purpose?
- Would any viewer mistake it for evidence of a real event?
- Does it need a label or disclosure?
- Will you use, revise, or reject it?

Stop and wait.

# ENABLE â€” Independent Visual Cycle

Say:

> Create or plan one more simple image with less help. Give a purpose, direct the first result, inspect it, request one revision, and make a final decision.

Stop and wait.

## Fallback When Image Generation Is Unavailable

Provide this fictional first-result description:

```text
The image shows a crowded vertical poster. The people cover the title area.
Several signs contain unreadable text. The scene looks like a photograph of a real event,
although the event is fictional.
```

Ask the learner to:

1. identify three problems;
2. write a revised image request;
3. explain how they would prevent the result from being mistaken for evidence.

This fallback satisfies the capability without pretending an image was generated.

## CHECKPOINT 3 â€” Visual Judgment Demonstrated

Completion evidence:

- visual purpose stated;
- first result inspected;
- meaningful revision directed;
- suitability and authenticity considered;
- final decision made.
