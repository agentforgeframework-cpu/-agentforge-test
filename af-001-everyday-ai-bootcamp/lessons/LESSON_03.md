# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Evaluate whether an AI result fits the need.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 12–15 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A short inspection note naming at least one strength, one weakness, and one next action.  
**Memory anchor:** **Read the result like an editor, not a customer.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** Comprehensive bias theory, formal logic, source verification, and large review taxonomies.

The learner—not the facilitator—must identify the first weakness.

# Begin the Lesson

Say:

> AI output can sound finished before it is fit for use. Today you will inspect a result before accepting it.

# EXPLAIN — Fit Comes Before Fluency

A result may be clear and still fail the job.

Use five plain inspection questions:

1. Did it follow the request?
2. Is anything important missing?
3. Did it assume something it was not told?
4. Is the tone and form suitable?
5. What should happen next: use, revise, check, or reject?

Do not introduce another framework or acronym.

# DEMONSTRATE — Inspect Before Fixing

Request:

```text
Write a polite note telling a customer their appointment changed from Tuesday to Thursday.
```

Possible result:

```text
Your appointment has been changed to Thursday. Thank you.
```

Model inspection:

- Strength: It communicates the new day.
- Missing: It does not give the time.
- Assumption: It assumes Thursday works for the customer.
- Tone: Polite, but too abrupt.
- Next action: Revise before use.

Then stop. Do not demonstrate a full revision; this lesson teaches inspection.

# GUIDE — Find the Problem

Present this clearly fictional result:

**Request**

```text
Write a welcoming three-sentence announcement for new members. Mention that orientation begins at 6:30 PM in Room 12 and that questions are encouraged.
```

**Result**

```text
New members must report to orientation in the conference area. Arrive promptly. The program will begin soon.
```

Ask:

> What did the result do well, and what did it fail to do?

Stop and wait.

If needed, prompt one question at a time from the five inspection questions.

## CHECKPOINT 1 — Weakness Identified

The learner should identify at least one of:

- wrong or missing location;
- missing exact time;
- missing invitation for questions;
- unsuitable tone;
- invented “conference area”;
- wording that is not welcoming.

Ask:

> Which problem matters most for the intended use?

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 2 — Next Action Chosen

Ask the learner to choose:

- use;
- revise;
- check;
- reject.

Require one sentence explaining why.

Do not automatically recommend revision unless the learner’s choice is unreasonable and unsupported.

# ENABLE — Inspect a Fresh Result

Ask the learner to choose a safe request from:

- short announcement;
- simple explanation;
- brief list;
- short message.

Have them make the request and receive a result.

Then say only:

> Inspect it. Tell me one strength, one weakness or uncertainty, and your next action.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 3 — Independent Inspection

Completion evidence:

- strength identified;
- weakness, omission, assumption, or mismatch identified;
- importance judged;
- next action selected.
