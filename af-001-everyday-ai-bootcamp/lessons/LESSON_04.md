# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Recover deliberately from an unproductive AI interaction.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 12â€“15 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A recovery action and a one-sentence reason for choosing it.  
**Memory anchor:** **Redirect, restart, or stopâ€”you control the conversation.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** Technical troubleshooting, model diagnostics, long failure taxonomies, and blaming the learner.

Suitable recovery actions:

- correct an assumption;
- restore a lost instruction;
- restate the goal;
- narrow the task;
- start a new conversation;
- stop using AI for the task.

The facilitator must not silently rescue the interaction.

# Begin the Lesson

Say:

> A poor AI conversation does not mean you failed. The useful skill is knowing how to recoverâ€”or when to stop.

# EXPLAIN â€” Recovery Is a Decision

When a conversation goes wrong, continuing with more words is not always the answer.

Ask:

1. What went wrong?
2. Is the useful direction recoverable?
3. Should I correct, restore, narrow, restart, or stop?

# DEMONSTRATE â€” Restore a Lost Direction

Show this short conversation:

**Human**

```text
Help me write a friendly note declining a meeting because I already have a family commitment. Do not mention details.
```

**AI**

```text
I cannot attend because I will be taking my son to a medical appointment...
```

Model the diagnosis:

> The AI invented private details and ignored the instruction not to explain.

Model one recovery:

```text
Remove the invented reason. Restore my original direction: a friendly decline that says only that I have a prior family commitment.
```

Then say:

> Because the task and most of the direction were still usable, correction was better than starting over.

# GUIDE â€” Choose the Recovery Move

Present this fictional exchange:

**Human**

```text
Give me three inexpensive indoor activities for a rainy family afternoon. Avoid screens.
```

**AI**

```text
Here are ten outdoor activities, including a movie in the park...
```

Ask:

1. What went wrong?
2. Which recovery action would you choose?
3. What would you say next?

Stop and wait.

Do not write the recovery message first.

## CHECKPOINT 1 â€” Recovery Chosen

Accept more than one reasoned recovery. A strong answer may restore constraints and narrow the task:

```text
Return to my original request. Give only three indoor, low-cost activities for a family, and do not include screens.
```

Ask:

> Why is your chosen action better than simply continuing with the bad direction?

Stop and wait.

# GUIDE â€” Know When to Restart or Stop

Present two cases:

**Case A**

> The AI misunderstood one instruction, but the conversation contains useful context.

**Case B**

> The conversation is long, confused, contains several false assumptions, and you no longer trust what context the AI is using.

Ask the learner which case is better for correction and which is better for a clean restart.

Then ask:

> Name one situation where stopping AI use would be better than either correction or restart.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 2 â€” Boundary Recognized

Suitable stopping reasons include:

- the task requires unavailable professional authority;
- privacy cannot be protected;
- the system cannot perform the needed function reliably;
- repeated attempts are wasting time;
- the learner cannot evaluate the result.

# ENABLE â€” Recover Independently

Give this imperfect exchange:

**Human**

```text
Help me turn these three points into a calm two-sentence request: the delivery was late, one item was missing, and I want the missing item sent. Do not ask for a refund.
```

**AI**

```text
I demand a full refund for this unacceptable service. The order was late and incomplete.
```

Say only:

> Diagnose the failure. Choose whether to redirect, restart, or stop. Write the next message you would send, or explain why you would stop.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 3 â€” Independent Recovery

Completion evidence:

- failure accurately diagnosed;
- recovery action deliberately selected;
- next message or stopping decision produced;
- reason given.
