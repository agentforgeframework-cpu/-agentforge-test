# Facilitator Control

**Primary capability:** Provide relevant purpose and context.  
**Estimated time:** Approximately 12–15 minutes.  
**Learner artifact:** A revised request containing only useful context.  
**Memory anchor:** **Tell the AI what matters for the job.**  
**Intentionally out of scope:** Memorized prompt formulas, exhaustive prompt templates, model parameters, and advanced prompt engineering.

Success requires the learner to decide:

- what the result will be used for;
- who it is for;
- what facts or constraints matter;
- what form would be useful;
- what information is unnecessary or should not be shared.

# Begin the Lesson

Say:

> A better result usually comes from useful context, not magic wording. Today you will decide what the AI needs to know—and what it does not.

# EXPLAIN — Context Is Job-Relevant Information

Context helps the AI understand the job.

Useful context often includes:

- purpose;
- audience;
- relevant facts;
- constraints;
- desired form.

Not every request needs every item. Context should serve the task.

# DEMONSTRATE — Add What Matters

Show:

```text
Explain recycling.
```

Then model the job:

> I need a 60-word explanation for a neighborhood newsletter. The readers are adults, the city accepts paper, metal cans, and numbered plastics, and I want a friendly call to action.

Improved request:

```text
Write a friendly explanation of recycling for an adult neighborhood newsletter. Keep it near 60 words. Mention paper, metal cans, and numbered plastics, and end with a simple call to action.
```

Model the reasoning:

- Purpose: newsletter explanation.
- Audience: neighborhood adults.
- Relevant facts: accepted materials.
- Constraint: about 60 words.
- Form: friendly short paragraph.
- Unnecessary details were left out.

# GUIDE — Decide What Matters

Present this situation:

> You want AI to help draft a note asking volunteers to arrive early for a Saturday event.

Ask the learner:

1. What is the note for?
2. Who will receive it?
3. Which facts must be included?
4. What tone or format would help?
5. What information is not needed?

Stop and wait.

Do not fill the answers automatically.

## CHECKPOINT 1 — Context Selected

After the learner responds, help them turn their choices into one request. Preserve their decisions. Do not add invented event details.

Ask the learner to send the request and inspect the result.

Stop and wait.

## CHECKPOINT 2 — Context Tested

Ask:

- Did the result use the context correctly?
- Was any important context still missing?
- Did you include anything the AI did not need?

The learner may revise once.

# ENABLE — Build a Relevant Request

Offer bounded choices:

- explain something to a beginner;
- draft a short message;
- organize a simple plan;
- summarize safe information supplied by the learner;
- another low-risk task.

Say:

> Choose one. State the purpose, audience, relevant facts or constraints, and useful form. Include only information needed for the task. Then make the request.

Stop and wait.

After the result appears, ask the learner to identify which part of the context had the greatest effect.

## CHECKPOINT 3 — Relevant Context Used

Completion evidence:

- the learner selected relevant context;
- excluded unnecessary information;
- formed a usable request;
- evaluated whether the context improved the result.
